The Production Over Expected (POE) Framework

Traditional college stats lie. A receiver catching 80 balls in the SEC isn't the same as 80 balls in the MAC. A running back rushing for 1,200 yards behind an elite offensive line isn't the same as doing it behind a bottom-tier unit.

POE adjusts raw college production for contextual factors — conference strength, offensive line quality, QB talent, surrounding competition — to isolate what the prospect actually did vs. what anyone in their position would have done.

This became the highest-weighted variable in the WR model (over 50% of total weight), fundamentally proving that context-adjusted production is far more predictive than raw stats.

What POE Adjusts For

Conference Strength
SEC production weighted differently than Group of 5
O-Line Quality
RB rushing yards adjusted for blocking grades
QB Talent
WR production adjusted for passer quality
Surrounding Competition
Target share context — was he WR1 by default?

RB Archetype Framework

After the model initially undervalued speed backs like De'Von Achane, the RB evaluation was rebuilt with archetype-specific criteria:

Zone Backs
Vision, patience, one-cut ability. Key metric: yards after contact per attempt
Power Backs
Contact balance, short-yardage conversion. Key metric: broken tackle rate
Receiving Backs
Route-running, catch radius. Key metric: yards per route run
Speed Backs
Breakaway rate, acceleration. Key metric: speed build ratio

The GENERATIONAL Label

GENERATIONAL is reserved only for prospects who truly break the model — players whose POE is so far above the regression line that the model can't explain them with contextual factors alone. In the 2018-2023 training data, only a handful of prospects earned this designation.
